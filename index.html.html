About the CDO Community Eco-Guide

The CDO Community Eco-Guide is a community information project designed to help residents find barangay-related environmental information, understand waste segregation, and locate official city channels for questions and concerns.

This is an independent information guide, not an official City Government website. Barangay-specific collection schedules and announcements should only be treated as confirmed when published or verified by the relevant barangay office or the City Local Environment and Natural Resources Office (CLENRO).

A Brief History of Cagayan de Oro

The City Government’s historical account records evidence of early communities in the area, including archaeological discoveries at Huluga. The settlement was formerly known as Cagayan de Misamis before it became the City of Cagayan de Oro.

Early settlementArchaeological evidence from Huluga points to human habitation in the area centuries ago.
December 17, 1949House Bill No. 54, proposing the creation of the City of Cagayan de Oro, was introduced in Congress.
June 15, 1950President Elpidio Quirino signed the city charter, officially creating Cagayan de Oro as a city.
Meaning of “de Oro”The city’s official history explains that “de Oro” refers to gold-mining activity in hinterland areas known to Spanish explorers.

History summary based on the City Government of Cagayan de Oro’s published history and 2023 Ecological Profile. Read the official city history and 2023 Ecological Profile (PDF) for the full account.

Proper Waste Segregation

Separate waste at the point where it is generated.

🍌

Biodegradable

  • Food scraps
  • Fruit and vegetable waste
  • Leaves and grass
  • Other compostable materials
♻️

Recyclable

  • Clean paper
  • Cardboard
  • Glass containers
  • Suitable recyclable plastics
  • Metal cans
🗑️

Residual

  • Non-recyclable packaging
  • Contaminated materials
  • Other non-recoverable waste
⚠️

Special / Hazardous

  • Batteries
  • Electronic waste
  • Fluorescent lamps
  • Other hazardous household materials

Follow your barangay or city guidance for proper drop-off and handling.

RA 9003 / CITY ORDINANCE

🌏 Simple Things Every Household Can Do

01 — Segregate at source

Separate biodegradable, recyclable, residual and other waste before disposal.

02 — Follow collection schedules

Put waste out according to your barangay's current collection schedule.

03 — Reduce single-use materials

Bring reusable bags, bottles and containers whenever practical.

04 — Keep recyclables clean

Clean and separate recyclable materials before bringing them to an MRF or collection point.

05 — Compost when possible

Organic household waste can potentially be composted instead of becoming residual waste.

06 — Report collection problems

Contact the appropriate barangay or CLENRO channel when waste is not collected according to the official schedule.
